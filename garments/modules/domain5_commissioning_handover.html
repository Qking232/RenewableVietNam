T&C Technical Failure Modes, Mitigation Playbooks & Readiness Scorecard

Risk Mitigation Playbooks
Playbook A: Biomass Boiler Flue Gas Emission Spike GPMT Risk

Failure Mode: Particulates > 200 mg/Nm³ or CO spike during DONRE 72h environmental sampling.

• Root Cause: Biomass fuel moisture > 35% or electrostatic precipitator (ESP) field arcing.
• Immediate Action: Switch to certified kiln-dried wood pellets (<12% moisture); increase ESP primary voltage to 55kV; trim secondary overfire air dampers to achieve complete combustion.
Playbook B: Fire Booster Pump Flow Deficit (<3,800 L/min) PCCC C07 Risk

Failure Mode: Discharge pressure drops below 8.5 bar at hydraulic flow boundary during C07 test.

• Root Cause: Suction vortexing in reservoir or debris blockage in primary suction basket.
• Immediate Action: Clear suction strainers; verify anti-vortex plates inside 1,200 m³ reservoir; adjust diesel engine governor to rated 2,950 RPM full-load speed.
Playbook C: ETP Biological MBBR Bacteria Shock Wastewater Risk

Failure Mode: Dissolved oxygen collapse or biomass wash-out yielding effluent COD > 75 mg/L.

• Root Cause: Sudden toxic dye surge from laundry wash line or nutrient starvation during ramp-up.
• Immediate Action: Divert raw effluent to 800 m³ emergency pond; inject seed sludge from neighboring industrial brewery; dose urea/diammonium phosphate (DAP) nutrients to re-establish biological growth.
Playbook D: Protective Relay Tripping upon 22kV Energization Power Grid Risk

Failure Mode: SEL-751 overcurrent trip upon closing main vacuum circuit breaker (VCB).

• Root Cause: Transformer inrush current transient mistriggering instantaneous 50P protection.
• Immediate Action: Activate 2nd harmonic restraint in SEL-751 settings (15% threshold); stagger energization of transformer 1 and transformer 2 by 15 minutes; coordinate trip curves with PC Bến Tre dispatch center.

Scenario 1: Managing Contractor vs. Client Operations Interface Clashes in Month 13

Operations Handover
The Executive Interview Question: "The General Contractor is performing final live testing of MEP in the factory hall, but your Country GM wants to move 200 sewing machines and operational staff in early to hit apparel buyer delivery schedules. How do you manage this conflict without invalidating contractor warranties or risking safety?"
Model Project Director Answer:

"I implement a structured Partial Taking-Over (Sectional Completion) Protocol under FIDIC Clause 10.2. We do not allow uncontrolled co-occupancy. Instead, I divide the 35,000 m² footprint into defined spatial and temporal zones:

  1. Sectional Handover of Production Hall: Issue a Partial Taking-Over Certificate for the sewing floor only after life safety (ESFR sprinklers, smoke spill, emergency exits) has passed 100% C07 inspection.
  2. Custody & Warranty Boundary Demarcation: The client’s operations team formally assumes utility operating costs and risk of damage for that specific zone, while contractor warranties for plant equipment (Chillers, Boilers, ETP) remain protected under the main MEP contract.
  3. Physical Isolation & Dual-Lock LOTO: Heavy MEP utility rooms (22kV substation, boiler house, ETP) remain under sole contractor custody with physical security turnstiles and red-tag LOTO. Operations staff are legally barred from entry until full COD."

Scenario 2: Contractor Demands Taking-Over Certificate (TOC) with Open Snag List

FIDIC Clause 10.1
The Executive Interview Question: "The contractual Completion Date has arrived. The contractor has achieved functional operation, but 35 punch-list items remain incomplete. The contractor demands you issue the Taking-Over Certificate (TOC) immediately so they avoid $14,500/day Liquidated Damages. How do you respond?"
Model Project Director Answer:

"Under FIDIC Yellow/Red Book Sub-Clause 10.1, the Engineer shall issue the Taking-Over Certificate only when the Works have been completed in accordance with the Contract, except for minor outstanding work and defects which will not substantially affect the use of the Works for their intended purpose.

  1. Enforce Category A Zero Tolerance: I review the 35 snags against our classification matrix. If even a single snag is Category A (affecting life safety, environmental compliance, or factory operability), the TOC is legally withheld. Under Clause 8.7, Liquidated Damages ($14,500/day) continue to accrue legitimately because the facility cannot be safely used.
  2. Conditioned TOC for Category B Only: If all 35 items are proven to be Category B (minor cosmetic blemishes, labeling, touch-up paint), I issue the TOC to halt Liquidated Damages, but attach a legally binding 14-day Undertaking of Rectification.
  3. Financial Retention Leverage: The 5% Retention Money ($725,000) or an equivalent Snag Retention deduction is strictly held. If the contractor fails to complete the snags within 14 days, Clause 11.4 authorizes me to hire a third-party subcontractor to execute the work and back-charge the contractor’s retention."

Scenario 4: Safety Control during 22kV Energization with 200 Finishing Workers on Site

Zero Harm HSE
The Executive Interview Question: "You are scheduled to energize the 22kV substation from the EVN grid at 09:00 AM on Sunday, but civil sub-contractors still have 200 finishing painters and ceiling workers inside the factory. How do you guarantee zero electrical shock incidents?"
Model Project Director Answer:

"I execute a non-negotiable High-Voltage Energization Safety Protocol based on physical exclusion and absolute operational segregation:

  1. Complete Red-Zone Lockdown: The electrical substation building and transformer yard are placed under 100% physical lockdown 24 hours prior to energization. Only certified High-Voltage Switching License holders, EVN dispatch engineers, and my Lead Electrical PM are granted access.
  2. Downstream Distribution Bus Isolation (LOTO): All downstream low-voltage 400V main distribution board (MDB) breakers feeding the factory floor are mechanically padlocked in the OFF / RACKED-OUT position under my personal custody lock. Even when the 22kV transformers are energized, zero live voltage can reach the factory hall where finishing trades are working.
  3. Mandatory Site Stand-Down & Horn Warning: Conduct a mandatory 15-minute site-wide safety briefing for all 200 workers at 08:00 AM. Sound audible sirens 10 minutes and 1 minute prior to closing the main 22kV vacuum circuit breaker, with security guards stationed every 20 meters along the substation perimeter."
